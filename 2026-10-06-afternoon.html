Denmark's Entire Population Register Breached — 8.8 Million Records Exposed


Oct 6, 06:00


Attackers abused a private company's legitimate access rights to Denmark's Central Person Register (CPR) to exfiltrate names, addresses, and national identification numbers for 8.8 million people — effectively the entire Danish population, living and dead. The breach required no technical exploit; it exploited a trusted third-party access channel, a pattern that is increasingly common in large-scale government data compromises. Denmark's digitalization ministry has advised citizens never to share their CPR number in response to unsolicited contact.


A nation-scale identity record breach via trusted third-party access is a textbook example of the third-party risk vector — directly relevant for any organization that grants external parties privileged access to sensitive data.
