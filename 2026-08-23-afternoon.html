Wi-Fi 8 Prioritizes Reliability and Latency Over Speed — A Fundamental Design Shift


Aug 23, 06:41


Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn), drawing 118 Hacker News points and 90 comments, is the first Wi-Fi generation explicitly designed around reliability, deterministic latency, and reduced interference rather than peak throughput. The standard targets congested multi-device home and enterprise environments where too many devices competing for the same spectrum creates real-world performance collapse despite fast theoretical speeds. Key features include multi-AP coordination and improved spectrum sharing — changes that matter for IoT-dense deployments, smart buildings, and hybrid work infrastructure.


A Wi-Fi standard that trades raw speed for reliability is a meaningful architectural shift for anyone planning network infrastructure — the implications extend from enterprise Wi-Fi deployments to IoT and smart building design.
