Head Mare Hackers Breach TrueConf, Trojanize Client Installers With Backdoors


Aug 8, 24:00


The Head Mare hacktivist group exploited unpatched vulnerabilities in TrueConf video conferencing servers to replace legitimate client installers with malicious versions delivering backdoors to end users who downloaded and installed the software. The attack is a classic supply chain compromise — users trusting official-looking downloads from a vendor's own infrastructure receive malware. TrueConf is widely deployed in enterprise and government environments, particularly in Russian-speaking markets.


A trojanized installer attack on enterprise video conferencing infrastructure is an active supply chain threat — any organization running TrueConf should verify installer integrity and patch server-side immediately.

RovoBlast: New Atlassian Rovo Attack Method Steals Confluence, Jira, and SharePoint Data With One Click


Aug 8, 24:00


Varonis researchers identified RovoBlast, a one-click attack method against Atlassian's Rovo AI assistant that could be exploited to exfiltrate data from Confluence, Jira, and connected SharePoint environments. This is distinct from the prompt injection attack path covered in prior briefs — RovoBlast represents an additional attack surface in Rovo requiring only a single user interaction to trigger. Atlassian has been notified and the vulnerability has been addressed, but organizations should confirm patch status across all Rovo deployments.


A second distinct attack method against Rovo — now confirmed by SecurityWeek alongside the earlier prompt injection research — means Atlassian's AI assistant has multiple active attack surfaces that enterprise security teams must account for.
