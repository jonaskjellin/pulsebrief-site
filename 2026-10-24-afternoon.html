CISA Adds Collabora Online and Arista VeloCloud Flaws to KEV — Federal Agencies Must Patch Immediately


Oct 24, 06:25


CISA has added two actively exploited critical vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: CVE-2026-110447 in Collabora Online Built-in CODE Server (CVSS 9.2) and the Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator SQL injection flaw previously reported as a third zero-day in four months. Federal agencies face mandatory remediation deadlines, and the KEV listing confirms exploitation is occurring in the wild rather than theoretically possible. The VeloCloud addition is particularly significant given the platform's repeated exploitation pattern.


CISA KEV additions carry mandatory remediation requirements for federal agencies and serve as the authoritative signal that exploitation is confirmed — any organization running either platform should treat patching as urgent.

Node2Shell Expands to HTTP2Shell — Blast Radius Now Covers Python HTTP Libraries and Apps via Brotli CVE


Oct 24, 08:06


The Node2Shell vulnerability (CVE-2026-111497) in the Brotli decompression library has expanded beyond Node.js to affect Python packages and HTTP libraries, with researchers at Aikido Security documenting that many Python-based HTTP applications share the same vulnerable Brotli code path. The expanded blast radius — now branded HTTP2Shell — means the attack surface is significantly larger than initially disclosed, covering a wide range of Python web applications, not just Node.js deployments. Any team running Python HTTP libraries or applications should audit their Brotli dependency chain immediately.


An RCE vulnerability that has expanded from Node.js to Python HTTP libraries now threatens a vastly larger portion of the web application ecosystem — patch prioritization must be revisited.

Eye Security Pushed 2M+ Updates to Remotely Disable Popcorn Time Botnet Risk Across Devices It Did Not Own


Oct 24, 09:04


Dutch firm Eye Security has disclosed that between November 2025 and March 2026 it pushed over 2 million unauthorized updates to third-party computers running the abandoned Time4Popcorn client, disabling a privileged updater that ran over unencrypted HTTP and could have allowed anyone who registered abandoned domains to execute code on millions of machines. Researchers registered the abandoned domains themselves and used the updater mechanism to neutralize the risk before malicious actors could exploit it. The disclosure raises unresolved legal and ethical questions about unauthorized access to third-party systems, even for defensive purposes.


The ethical and legal precedent of a security firm pushing unsanctioned code to millions of devices it doesn't own — even defensively — is as significant as the original botnet risk and sets a contested precedent for future defensive interventions.

Oct 24, 10:36


Google has updated its privacy policy to explicitly confirm that connecting Gmail, Google Photos, or other personal data services to AI features in Search or Gemini Apps enables that data to be used for personalized advertising — reversing a long-standing commitment that those services would remain walled off from ad targeting. The policy change, now formally documented in The Hacker News following the earlier Verge report, has triggered significant user and regulatory attention. Users who have enabled AI Mode in Search or connected Gemini Apps to personal data are now subject to ad targeting based on email content and personal photos.


This is the formal documentation of a major privacy policy reversal at Google affecting billions of users — any organization advising on data governance, privacy compliance, or enterprise Google Workspace usage needs to understand the new terms.
